This policy is an internal management-system policy. It does not constitute an external certification, attestation, appraisal or market ranking.

## 1. Purpose

ARBM SIST operates an integrated management system covering quality, information security, AI governance, IT service management, privacy, software/product quality, continuity, supplier governance and evidence integrity.

## 2. Core commitments

ARBM SIST will:

1. define measurable quality and service objectives and review them periodically;
2. protect confidentiality, integrity and availability of information using risk-based controls;
3. govern AI systems and AI providers through documented ownership, risk assessment, traceability, human oversight and lifecycle controls;
4. preserve evaluator independence and provenance for benchmark and certification evidence;
5. maintain fail-closed evidence handling and immutable technical references where feasible;
6. comply with applicable contractual, legal, regulatory and certification requirements within the agreed scope;
7. manage privacy risks and personal-data processing through defined lifecycle controls;
8. manage incidents, problems, changes, releases and service continuity through documented processes;
9. evaluate suppliers/providers according to security, reliability, continuity, privacy and service criteria;
10. prohibit silent paid fallback where ZERO_SPEND HARD applies;
11. keep heavy local execution at zero where remote-only execution is mandated;
12. investigate nonconformities and incidents using root-cause analysis and corrective action;
13. conduct internal audits and management reviews based on real operating evidence;
14. continually improve the integrated management system and the product based on evidence, customer needs, incidents, audit findings and objective metrics.

## 3. Quality management principles

The management system maintains:

- defined customer and stakeholder requirements;
- measurable quality objectives;
- process ownership;
- controlled changes;
- retained evidence of verification and validation;
- corrective actions for verified nonconformities;
- periodic review of process effectiveness.

## 4. Information security principles

Security governance includes:

- risk assessment and treatment;
- asset and information inventory;
- access control and least privilege;
- secrets management;
- secure software lifecycle controls;
- vulnerability and dependency management;
- logging and monitoring;
- incident response;
- continuity and recovery;
- supplier security review;
- evidence retention and chain of custody.

## 5. AI governance principles

AI governance includes:

- inventory of AI systems/models/providers;
- intended-use and prohibited-use definitions;
- risk and impact assessment;
- provider admission criteria;
- traceability of model/provider versions when material;
- human oversight for high-impact decisions;
- monitoring of reliability, safety and performance;
- failover governance;
- documentation of known limitations;
- separation between system under evaluation and official evaluator where required.

## 6. Privacy principles

Privacy governance includes:

- data inventory and processing purposes;
- role classification for controller/processor responsibilities where applicable;
- lawful-basis or contractual-basis mapping as applicable;
- data minimization;
- retention and deletion rules;
- data-subject request handling where applicable;
- privacy risk assessment;
- processor/subprocessor governance;
- incident/breach handling.

## 7. IT service management principles

Service governance includes:

- service catalogue and ownership;
- service-level objectives where applicable;
- change/release control;
- incident and problem management;
- continuity and availability planning;
- capacity and supplier management;
- continual improvement based on measurable service evidence.

## 8. Software/product quality principles

Product-quality assessment uses objective criteria aligned, where relevant, to ISO/IEC 25010 product-quality characteristics. Internal evidence must remain distinct from independent third-party assessment.

## 9. Evidence integrity and external claims

The following rules are mandatory:

- documentation alone is not implementation evidence;
- no backdated approvals or fabricated historical operating records;
- historical benchmark results remain tied to the exact release/SHA under which they were produced;
- no ISO, SOC 2, CMMI, OSWorld Verified or similar external claim may be made before the corresponding independent body issues or publishes it;
- no `Top 3 worldwide` or equivalent market-ranking claim may be published without a comparable independent benchmark or recognized external publication establishing that position.

## 10. Approval and review

This policy is effective from 2026-09-17. It must be reviewed at least annually and after material changes to scope, regulatory obligations, major incidents, benchmark rules or external-certification requirements.
